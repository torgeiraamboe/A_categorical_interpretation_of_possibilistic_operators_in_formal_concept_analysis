\section{Compositions and closures}

We have now constructed the eight possibilistic operators in formal concept analysis, as introduced in \citeme, by simple categorical constructions associated to the underlying boolean profunctor $R\: G \nrightarrow G$. It remains then, to study the different possible compositions of upper-case and lower-case possibilistic operators. 

Given our eight operators, there are $16$ possible compositions that produce an endofunctor on $[G\op, \2]$. As we only consider endofunctors on $[G\op, \2]$ -- the ones on $[M,\2]\op$ are completely analagous -- we will use the combined notation $(-)^\sigma \circ (-)_\rho := (-)^\sigma_\rho$ for simplicity. We then get the following table of compositions:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 & (-)_\Delta & (-)_\Pi & (-)_N & (-)_\nabla \\ \hline
(-)^\Delta
    & (-)^\Delta_\Delta
    & (-)^\Delta_\Pi
    & (-)^\Delta_N
    & (-)^\Delta_\nabla \\[0.3em]

(-)^\Pi
    & (-)^\Pi_\Delta
    & (-)^\Pi_\Pi
    & (-)^\Pi_N
    & (-)^\Pi_\nabla \\[0.3em]

(-)^N
    & (-)^N_\Delta
    & (-)^N_\Pi
    & (-)^N_N
    & (-)^N_\nabla \\[0.3em]

(-)^\nabla
    & (-)^\nabla_\Delta
    & (-)^\nabla_\Pi
    & (-)^\nabla_N
    & (-)^\nabla_\nabla
\end{array}
\]

By our construction of the operators we have two adjunctions associated to the profunctor $R$. We have the FCA closure adjunction,
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{[G\op, \2]} \arrow[r, yshift = 2pt, "(-)^\Delta"] & {[M,\2]\op} \arrow[l, yshift = -2pt, "(-)_\Delta"]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
defined by the right Kan extension, and we have the adjunction defined by the left Kan extension,
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{[G\op, \2]} \arrow[r, yshift = 2pt, "(-)^\Pi"] & {[M,\2]\op} \arrow[l, yshift = -2pt, "(-)_N"].
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
We also have the two adjunctions associated to the complement profunctor $\oR\: G\to M$: 
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{[G\op, \2]} \arrow[r, yshift = -2pt, "(-)^\nabla",swap] & {[M,\2]\op} \arrow[l, yshift = 2pt, "(-)_\nabla",swap].
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
and 
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{[G\op, \2]} \arrow[r, yshift = -2pt, "(-)^N", swap] & {[M,\2]\op} \arrow[l, yshift = 2pt, "(-)_\Pi", swap],
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
where due to the use of $\neg$, the adjunctions are flipped after moving to the correct concept lattice. 

These latter two, then, form \emph{interior operators}, as we can see from \cref{prop:closure-interior}. The goal of this section is to prove that the former two are the \emph{only} two of the $16$ compositions that form closure operators on $P(G)$. 

\subsection{Idempotency and closure}

By definition, a closure operator is extensive, monotone and idempotent. For an operator $F$, this means that
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $A\subseteq F(A)$,
    \item $A\subseteq A' \implies F(A)\subseteq F(A')$, and
    \item $FF(A) = F(A)$, respectively.
\end{enumerate}

The FCA adjunction coming from a formal context $(G,M,R)$ satisfies all tree of these. By \citeme, the monad $R_*R^*$ is idempotent, and the two other properties follow formally by the adjunction unit and the fact that it is a functor on posets. Thus, any monadic functor on a poset satisfies these, see \citeme.  
% 2.1.11 in https://academicworks.cuny.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7356&context=gc_etds

In order to be recognized as a FCA closure operator on \emph{some} formal context, these three axioms have to be fulfilled, otherwise the resulting fixedpoints are not formal concepts. The following is rather trivial then, but we state it for reference later.

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:axioms_for_closure}
    Let $L\: \C \to \C$ be an endofunctor on a poset. If $L$ fails to be extensive, monotone or idempotent, then there is no formal context $(G,M,R)$ such that $\mathrm{Fix}(L) \simeq \mathrm{Nuc}(R)$. 
\end{proposition}

This result will help us quickly identify which of the asymmetric compositions later possibly could give rise to alternative formal concepts on some related formal context. However, satisfying these three properties does not, as far as we know, guarantee that the fixedpoints form the formal concept of some formal context. 

\begin{remark}
    Dually, given an adjoint pair $L\dashv R$, the composition $LR$ is a comonad. On posets, this comonad satisfies dual properties of the closure operators, called being an \emph{interior} operator. In the case of formal concepts, instead of completing a subset $A$ to a formal concept, i.e., constructing the smallest formal concept containing the set, the interior operator finds the largest formal concept \emph{inside} $A$. 
\end{remark}

\subsection{Binary compositions}

We can now classify exactly which binary compositions of the eight operators that give closure operators and interior operators. 

\begin{theorem}
    If $F$ is an operator in table X, then $F$ is a closure operator only if it is the monad of one of the four adjunctions. 
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    The monads of the adjunctions are always closure operators, but due to the use of $\neg$, two of them are in effect interior operators. In particular, $(-)^\Delta_\Delta$ and $(-)^N_\Pi$ are closure operators, while $(-)^\Pi_N$ and $(-)^\nabla_\nabla$ are interior operators -- see \cref{prop:closure-interior}. Hence, we only need to check the remaining $12$ cases. We check these by simple counter examples, using \cref{prop:axioms_for_closure} to test whether $F$ satisfies the properties. In fact, it will be enough to test whether $F$ is extensive. 

    First, let $G=\{a,b\}$, $M=\{x,y\}$ and $R$ given by $aRx, bRy$. Chose the subset $A=G$. Then we have $A^\Delta = \emptyset$, $A^\Pi = \{x,y\}$, $A^N=\emptyset$ and $A^\nabla = \emptyset$. In this example, both $A^\Delta_\Pi = \emptyset$ and $A^\Delta_N = \emptyset$, showing that neither operators $(-)^\Delta_\Pi$ or $(-)^\Delta_N$ are closure operators. Similarly, we get $A^\Pi_\Delta = \emptyset$, $A^\Pi_\nabla=\emptyset$, $A^N_\Delta = \emptyset$, $A^N_\nabla = \emptyset$, $A^\Delta_\Pi = \emptyset$ and $A^\nabla_N = \emptyset$, showing that none of these can be closure operators as well. 

    It remains to show the same for $(-)^\Pi_\Pi$, $(-)^\Delta_\nabla$, $(-)^N_N$ and $(-)^\nabla_\Delta$. For the former two we use the formal context $G=\{a\}$, $M=\emptyset$ and $A=G$. This gives $A^\Pi_\Pi = \emptyset$ and $A^\Delta_\nabla = \emptyset$, showing neither is extensive and hence not a closure operator. Similarly, using $G=\{a\}$, $M=\{x\}$, $aRx$ and $A=G$ $A^\nabla_\Delta = \emptyset$. For the final operator, $(-)^N_N$ we use the context $G=\{a,b\}$, $M=\{x\}$, $aRx$, $bRx$ and $A=\{a\}$. This gives $A^N_N = \emptyset$, which also finishes the proof. 
\end{proof}

We round of this section by using the categorical machinery to identify a sequence of new types of concepts, or pairs, that naturally arise from the framework. These come from the following observation: Given two adjunctions 
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
    \C \arrow[r, yshift=2pt, "L_1"] & \D \arrow[r, yshift=2pt, "L_2"] \arrow[l, yshift=-2pt, "R_1"] & \E \arrow[l,yshift=-2pt, "R_2"]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
the composed functor $F_2\circ F_1$ is a left adjoint to $G_2\circ G_1$. Recall from \cref{prop:axioms_for_closure} that on complete lattices, closure operators are equivalent to monads. 

On $P(G)$ we have two left adjoints, $(-)^\Delta$ and $(-)^\Pi$. Similarly, on $P(M)\op$ we also have two left adjoints, $(-)_\Pi$ and $(-)_\nabla$. Hence, any arbitrary composition of these will produce a closure operator on $P(G)$ by composing with the corresponding adjoints. 

\begin{example}
    The functor 
    \[(-)^\Delta\circ (-)_\Pi \circ (-)^\Delta \: [G\op, \2]\to [M,\2]\op\]
    is a composition of left adjoints, hence is a left adjoint itself with right adjoint given by 
    \[(-)_\Delta\circ (-)^N \circ (-)_\Delta \: [M, \2]\op\to [G\op,\2].\]
    The composition of these is by \cref{prop:axioms_for_closure} a closure operator. 
\end{example}

We believe that the corresponding concepts of these closure operators would be interesting to study, as they are truly \emph{new} types of concepts.
