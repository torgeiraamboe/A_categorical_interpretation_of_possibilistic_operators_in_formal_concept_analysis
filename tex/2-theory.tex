

\section{Category theory and FCA}

Formal concept analysis has attracted interest by category theorists for being a natural and useful application of their abstract tools. Category theory was introduced by Eilenberg and MacLane in \citeme, and is an abstract mathematical theory for understanding compositional structure -- see \citeme for a comprehensive introduction. 

A category consists of \emph{objects} and \emph{morphisms}, which are abstract relationships between objects. Category theory can then be thought of as studying objects by understanding how they relate to all other objects of the same defined type. We will assume that the reader is familiar with basic categorical constructions. 

For a category theorist, the objects $G$ and attributes $M$ can be viewed as discrete categories. Any relation can be described as a $\2 = \{0,1\}$-valued profunctor, also called \emph{boolean} profunctors. In our case the profunctor $R\: G\nrightarrow M$ is a functor $G\op\times M\to \2$, which takes a pair $(g,m)$ to $1$ if $g$ has the attribute $m$ -- see \citeme for details. This will be our running definition of a formal context. 

\begin{definition}
    A \emph{formal context} $(G,M,R)$ consists of two discrete categories $G$ and $M$ and a boolean profunctor $R\: G\nrightarrow M$. We will say an object $g\in G$ has attribute $m\in M$ if $R(g,m)=1$, usually written $gRm$. 
\end{definition}

From this perspective we can also produce the formal concepts associated to any formal context. 

\subsection{The closure operators}

Fix a formal context $(G,M,R)$. To construct the associated formal concepts, notice that a the profunctor $R$ is by currying equivalent to two functors 
\[R^{-}\: G \to [M, \2]\op \text{ and } R_{-}\: M \to [G\op, \2].\]
We also have two Yoneda embeddings, giving a diagram
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
G \arrow[r, "R^{-}"] \arrow[d, "y"'] & {[M,\2]\op} \\
{[G\op, \2]} & M \arrow[l, "R_{-}"] \arrow[u, "cy"']       
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
where $y$ is the Yoneda embedding and $cy$ is the Yoneda coembedding into the presheaf and opcopresheaf categories respectively. As $G$ and $M$ are discrete, the functor categories are precicely the powersets of $G$ and $M$ respectively: $[G\op, \2] = P(G)$ and $[M, \2]\op = P(M)\op$. 

As $[G\op, \2]$ is complete, there is a unique colimit preserving right Kan extension $R^*$ of $R^-$ along the Yoneda embedding $y$, which we claim is the standard FCA closure on $P(G)$. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:upper-Delta}
    The right Kan extension $R^*$ of $R^-$ is the standard FCA closure operator $(-)^\Delta$. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The functor $R^*$ is given explicitly by using a categorical end-formula, which for a presheaf $A\in [G\op, \2]$ is
    \[R^*(A)(m) = \int_{g\in G} \2(A(g), gRm).\]
    In $\2$, the internal hom is given by \emph{implication}, and the above end-formula is computed pointwise, reducing it to the conjunction 
    \[R^*(A)(m) = \bigwedge_{g\in G}(A(g) \implies gRm).\]
    In other words, having $m\in R^*(A)$ is equivalent to having that for all $g$, if $g\in A$ then $g$ has the attribute $m$. This is precisely the FCA closure operation $(-)^\Delta = (-)'$ on $P(G)$ as defined earlier. 
\end{proof}

Similarly, the other curried functor $R_{-}$ has a right Kan extension $R_*$ along the coYoneda embedding $cY\: M\to [M,\2]\op$, which we claim is the other standard FCA closure operation. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:lower-Delta}
    The right Kan extension $R_*$ of $R_-$ is the standard FCA closure operator $(-)_\Delta$. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The proof is completely analagous to \cref{lm:upper-Delta}, as the right Kan extension has the formula,
    \[R_*(B)(g)=\int_{m\in M}\2(B(m), gRm).\]
    Hence, the same argument works and we recognize $R_*$ as $(-)_\Delta$.
\end{proof}

These two functors are not unrelated, but form an adjoint pair on the powerset categories. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:adjoint-Delta}
    The two operators $R^*$ and $R_*$ form an adjoint pair
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
    {[G\op, \2]} \arrow[r, "R^*", yshift=2pt] & {[M, \2]\op} \arrow[l, "R_*", yshift=-2pt].
    \end{tikzcd}
    \end{center}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    First, as $R^*$ preserves colimits, it has a right adjoint. For this adjoint to be $R_*$ we need to show that for any $A\in [G\op, \2]$ and $B\in [M, \2]\op$, we have $R^*(A)\subseteq B$ if and only if $A\subseteq R_*(B)$. 

    By the definition of $R^*$, we have $R^*(A)\subseteq B$ if and only if for all $m\in M$, 
    \[\bigwedge_{g\in G}(A(g)\implies gRm)\subseteq B(m).\]
    In $\2$, the join is the universal, hence this is equivalent to the statement that 
    \[\forall m\in M, \forall g\in G (A(g)\implies gRm) \subseteq B(m).\]
    This is again equivalent to 
    \[\forall g\in G, A(g) \subseteq \forall m\in M (B(m) \implies gRm).\]
    which we recognize as 
    \[\forall g\in G, A(g) \subseteq \bigwedge_{m\in M} (B(m) \implies gRm),\]
    which by the defintion of $R_*$ is the statement $A\subseteq R_*(B)$, finishing the proof. 
\end{proof}

We can now, as is standard in categorical FCA, recognize the formal concepts of $(G,M,R)$ as the fixedpoints of the adjunction $R^*\dashv R_*$, often called the \emph{nucleus} of the profunctor $R$, see \citeme. 

More formally, the composition $R_*\circ R^*$ is a monad on $P(G)$, and the extents of the formal concepts are the objects in the Eilenberg--Moore category of this monad. Similarly, taking opposite categories gives a monad $R^{*, \mathrm{op}}\circ R_*\op$ on $P(M)$, and its Eilenberg--Moore category gives the intents of the formal concepts. The nucleus consists of pairs of such objects and attributes, i.e., an object in $\mathrm{Nuc}(R)$ is of the form $(A,B)$ where $A = R_*(R*(B))$ and $B=R^{*, \mathrm{op}}(R_*\op(A))$. By adjunction, however, these determine each other, so we can focus only on one of them, choosing throughout the paper to focus on the object side. 

To summarize, the formal concepts arise completely naturally via simple categorical constructions from the profunctor $R$, via the fixedpoints of the monad $R_*R^*$ on $[G\op, \2]$, defined using right Kan extensions of the two associated curryings. 

\section{Two more operators}

Above we defined the functors $R^*$ and $R_*$ as the right Kan extensions of the two curried functors $R^-$ and $R_-$ along the respective Yoneda embeddings. This prompts the idea to also consider the \emph{left} Kan extensions. This will give us another two of the eight operations described by Dubois--Prade. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:upper-Pi}
    Given a formal context $R\: G\nrightarrow M$, the left Kan extension $R_!$ of the functor $R^-$ coincides with the possibilistic operator $(-)^\Pi$.  
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For the functor $R^-$, the left Kan extension is defined by the coend formula
    \[R_!(A)(m) = \int^{g\in G}(A(g)\otimes gRm).\]
    The monoidal structure in $\2$ is the \emph{and} operation, and as $[M,\2]\op$ is a complete lattice, the coend is the disjunction
    \[R_!(A)(m) = \bigvee_{g\in G}(A(G)\wedge gRm).\]
    In words, we have $m\in R_!(A)$ if and only if there is some $g\in A$ such that $g$ has the attribute $m$, which is precicely the definition of the operator $(-)^\Pi$.
\end{proof}

Similar to before, we also have the left Kan extension of the other curried functor $R_-$. But, as one perhaps should expect from \citeme, this is not $(-)_\Pi$ -- it is rather the possibilistic operator $(-)_N$. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:lower-N}
    The left Kan extension $R^!$ of the functor $R_-$ coincides with the possibilistic operator $(-)_N$. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    As for $R_!$, the left Kan extension is given by
    \[R^!(B)(g) = \int^{m\in M}(B(m)\otimes gRm),\]
    which is equivalent to 
    \[R^!(B)(g)=\bigvee_{m\in M}B(m)\wedge gRm.\]
    Using the boolean equivalence
    \[P\implies Q \iff \neg(P\wedge \neg Q)\]
    we get 
    \[R^!(B)(g)=\bigvee_{m\in M}\neg(B(m)\implies \neg gRm),\]
    which by De Morgan duality and the fact that $\neg gRm = r\oR m$, gives
    \[R^!(B)(g)=\neg\bigwedge_{m\in M}(B(m)\implies g\oR m).\]
\end{proof}

As for the other pair of functors $R^*$ and $R_*$, these also form an adjoint pair. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:adjoint-Pi-N}
    The left Kan extensions $R_!$ and $R^!$ of the two curried functors $R^-$ and $R_-$ form an adjoint pair
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
    {[G\op, \2]} \arrow[r, "R_!", yshift=2pt] & {[M, \2]\op} \arrow[l, "R^!", yshift=-2pt].
    \end{tikzcd}
    \end{center}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Since $R_!$ preserves all joins, it has a right adjoint. For this to be recognized as $R^!$ we need to show that for all $A\in [G\op,\2]$ and $B\in [M,\2]\op$ we have $A\subseteq R^!(B)$ if and only if $R_!(A)\subseteq B$. 
    
    By definition, holds if and only if for all $m\in M$ we have $\bigvee_g(A(g)\wedge gRm)\implies B(m)$, which holds if and only if for all $g\in G$ we have that if $g$ is in $A$, then $\bigwedge_m(gRm \implies B(m))$. This, we recognize as the definition of $(-)_N$, finishing the proof. 
\end{proof}


\subsection{The complement profunctor}

For any boolean profunctor $R$ one can also define is \emph{complement} $\oR\: G\op\times M \to \2$, defined by $g\oR m := \neg gRm$. This defines the \emph{complement} formal context $(G,M,\oR)$ as discussed in \citeme. 

As $\oR$ is a boolean profunctor in its own right, it also has two adjoint pairs, $\oR^*\vdash \oR_*$ and $\oR_!\vdash \oR^!$, coming from the left and right Kan extension as in \cref{lm:adjoint-Delta} and \cref{lm:adjoint-Pi-N}. We claim that these give rise to the remaining four possibilistic operators via a duality between operators on $(G,M,R)$ and operators on the complement context $(G,M,\oR)$.

The negation operator $\neg$ gives a natural duality operator on the presheaf category
\[\neg\: [G\op, \2]\to [G\op,\2]\op\]
defined by $(\neg A)(g) = \neg (A(g))$ \citeme. Any presheaf is in our case equivalent to a subset $A\subseteq G$, and the corresponding subset $\neg A$ is precicely all those $g$ not contained in $A$, also called its \emph{complement}. This also explains the contravariancy, as the complement flips the direction of inclusions of subsets. We have a completely analagous duality on $[M,\2]\op$, which we also denote $\neg$ for simplicity. 

Taking this negation dual twice is an equivalence, meaning $\neg$ is an \emph{involution}. Given an operator $F$ on $P(G)$, we will call precomposing with the complement operator the \emph{right dual} of $F$, and denote it $F\neg$. Similarly we define the \emph{left dual} to be $\neg F$. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:upper-N}
    The right dual of the operator $\oR^*$ coincides with the possibilistic operator $(-)^N$. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    By defintion, the functor is given pointwise by 
    \[\oR^*(\neg A)(m) = \bigwedge_{g\in G}(\neg A(g)\implies g\oR m).\] 
    Using the boolean equivalence 
    \[P\implies Q \iff \neg P \vee Q,\]
    which gives 
    \[\oR^*(\neg A)(m) = \bigwedge_{g\in G}(A(g)\vee g\oR m).\]
    Now, we can apply the fact that $\neg R = \oR$ together with the above boolean equivalence in reverse, which gives
    \[\oR^*(\neg A)(m) = \bigwedge_{g\in G}(gRm \implies A(g)),\]
    which is the definition of $(-)^N$. 
\end{proof}

Similarly, for the Kan extension of the other curried functor $\oR_-$ we obtain the operator $(-)_\Pi$.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:lower-Pi}
    The left dual $\neg\oR_*$ of the right Kan extension of the curried functor $\oR_-$, coincides with the possibilistic operator $(-)_\Pi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    By defintion we have
    \[\neg \oR_*(B)(g) = \neg \bigwedge_{m\in M}(B(m)\implies g\oR m),\]
    which by the boolean equivalence 
    \[P\implies \neg Q \iff \neg P\vee \neg Q\]
    gives 
    \[\neg \oR_*(\neg B)(g) = \neg \bigwedge_{m\in M}(k\neg B(m)\vee \neg gRm).\]
    Using De Morgan duality we have 
    \[\neg \oR_*(B)(g) = \bigvee_{m\in M}(B(m)\wedge gRm),\]
    which is the definition of $(-)_\Pi$. 
\end{proof}

From this we automatically obtain one of the main results from \citeme. The categorical viewpoint reveals exactly why this results appears, which was one of our motivations for this paper. 

\begin{corollary}
    The fixedpoints of the asymmetric composition $(-)_\Pi\circ (-)^N$ gives the formal concepts of the complement context $(G,M,\oR)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    The composition $(-)_\Pi\circ (-)^N$ is by \cref{lm:upper-N} and \cref{lm:lower-Pi} equivalent to $\neg \oR_* \circ \oR^* \neg$, which takes any subset $A$ to its complement, forms the formal concept -- in $[G\op,\2]\op$ the composition of the two functors changes order, and hence really gives the formal concept -- and finally takes the complement to the original lattice. The fixedpoints are then precicely the formal concepts in the complement lattice. 
\end{proof}

Now we have but two operators left, which arise from the two left Kan extensions associated to the two curryings of $\oR$. The proofs are essentially just using the defintions of the operators. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:upper-nabla}
    The right dual $\oR_!\neg$ of the left Kan extension of the curried functor $\oR^-$, is equivalent to the possibilistic operator $(-)^\nabla$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    By definition we have 
    \[\oR_!(\neg A)(m)=\bigvee_{g\in G} (\neg A(g)\wedge g\oR m).\]
    which we immediately recognize as the operator $(-)^\nabla$.   
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:lower-nabla}
    The right dual $\oR^!\neg$ of the left Kan extension of the curried functor $\oR_-$, is equivalent to the possibilistic operator $(-)_\nabla$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The proof is the same as \cref{lm:upper-nabla}. 
\end{proof}

To summarize then, we have four pairs of adjoint functors, arising from two boolean profunctors. This gives \cref{tab:lan-ran}, where we have denoted operators as either a right Kan extension (Ran) or a left Kan extension (Lan) and omitted the duals. 

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{l|ll}
& Ran & Lan\\
\hline
$R^-$   & $(-)^\Delta$ & $(-)^\Pi$ \\
$R_-$   & $(-)_\Delta$ & $(-)_N$  \\
$\oR^-$ & $(-)^N$      &  $(-)^\nabla$ \\
$\oR_-$ & $(-)_\Pi$    & $(-)_\nabla$
\end{tabular}
\caption{Possibilistic operators as Kan extensions}
\label{tab:lan-ran}
\end{table}



\subsection{Properties}

In \citeme, several properties and relations between the eight possibilistic operators were proven. In this section we reprove these from standard categorical properties. First, however, we prove that this categorical perspective also recovers the De Morgan dualities between these eight operators, as is their usual classical definitions in possibility theory -- the necessity is usually defined as $N(A) = 1-\Pi(\neg A)$, and similarly, $\nabla(A)=1-\Delta(\neg A)$. 

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lm:De-Morgan-duality}
    The four adjoint pairs of operators are related by De Morgan dualities: $(-)^N = \neg (-)^\Pi \neg$ and $(-)^\nabla = \neg (-)^\Delta \neg$, and similarly for the lower-case operators. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    We only prove the first relation, as the others follow by symmetry. 

    Let $A\in [G\op, \2]$ be a presheaf. By definition we have 
    \[\oR^*(A)(m) = \bigwedge_{g\in G}(A(g)\implies g\oR m).\]
    Using $g \oR m = \neg gRm$ and the boolean equivalence 
    \[P\implies \neg Q \iff \neg (P\wedge Q),\]
    we have 
    \[\oR^*(A)(m) = \bigwedge_{g\in G}\neg (A(g)\wedge g R m).\]
    This is, by De Morgan duality \citeme, equivalent to 
    \[\oR^*(A)(m) = \neg\bigvee_{g\in G}(A(g)\wedge g R m),\]
    which we can recognize as $\neg R_!$. 

    By \cref{lm:upper-N} and \cref{lm:upper-Pi} we have $(-)^N = \oR^*\neg$ and $(-)^\Pi = R_!$, giving 
    \[(-)^N = \oR^*\neg = \neg R_!\neg = \neg (-)^\Pi\neg,\]
    finishing the proof. 
\end{proof}

The following reproves \citeme. The proof uses some simple calculus of limits and colimits, which one needs to keep straight as there are a lot of opposite categories. 
%5.1 https://www.irit.fr/~Didier.Dubois/Papers0804/DDSCP_FI07.pdf 

\begin{proposition}[\citeme]
    Let $(G,M,R)$ be a formal context. For all subsets $A_1, A_2$ of $G$, we have: 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $(A_1\cup A_2)^\Delta = A_1^\Delta \cap A_2^\Delta$,
        \item $(A_1\cap A_2)^N = A_1^N \cap A_2^N$,
        \item $(A_1\cup A_2)^\Pi = A_1^\Pi \cup A_2^\Pi$, and
        \item $(A_1\cap A_2)^\nabla = A_1^\nabla \cup A_2^\nabla$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    We prove these one by one in the order presented. 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item As $(-)^\Delta\: [G\op, \2]\to [M,\2]\op$ is a left adjoint it preserves colimits, which in $[G\op,\2]$ are unions and in $[M,\2]\op$ are intersections. 
        \item The functor $(-)^N = \oR^*\neg$ is the dual of a left adjoint. As $\neg\: [G\op, \2]\to [G\op, \2]\op$ is an involution, it preserves both limits and colimits. Hence, intersections, which are limits in $[G\op, \2]$, are sent to colimits in $[M,\2]\op$, which are also intersections.
        \item Follows from (2) and the De Morgan duality of \cref{lm:De-Morgan-duality}.
        \item Follows from (1) and \cref{lm:De-Morgan-duality}. 
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

Similarly, we get the usual inclusion properties of these operators from simple categorical observations. 

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:closure-interior}
    Let $(G,M,R)$ be a formal context. For all subsets $A$ of $G$, we have: 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $A\subseteq A^\Delta_\Delta$
        \item $A\subseteq A^\Pi_N$
        \item $A^N_\Pi \subseteq A$
        \item $A^\nabla_\nabla \subseteq A$
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Again we prove these in the order presented. 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Follows immediately from the unit of the adjunction $\Id \to (-)_\Delta\circ (-)^\Delta$. 
        \item Same as (1), it follows from the adjunction unit of $(-)^\Pi \dashv (-)_N$. 
        \item We have $A^N_\Pi = \neg \oR_* \oR^*(A)\neg$, hence this also follows from the adjunction unit of $\oR^*\dashv \oR_*$ via the involution $\neg$. 
        \item Similar to (3), this follows from the adjunction unit or $\oR_!\dashv \oR^!$. 
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
